Professor Emeritus of Medicine and Public Health

Dr. Stephen J. Jay, MD

Indiana University School of Medicine  ·  Richard M. Fairbanks School of Public Health  ·  Indianapolis, Indiana
Dr. Stephen Jordan Jay, MD is one of the most consequential figures in Indiana public health over the last half century — a bedside pulmonologist for over 60 years, the founding chair of Indiana's first school of public health, a policy advocate who built the state's tobacco control infrastructure, and a medical historian who rescued from obscurity the Hoosier physician who first warned America about smoking and lung cancer. His PubMed-indexed record spans 104 papers across 40 years. His awards span clinical, academic, policy, and humanistic domains in equal measure.

Education and Training

Jay attended Wabash College in Crawfordsville, Indiana — a small, all-male liberal arts college with a strong tradition of producing physicians — before entering medical school. He graduated from the Indiana University School of Medicine (IUSM) in 1966 as an Alpha Omega Alpha honor graduate, the highest academic distinction in American medical education, reserved for approximately the top 10 percent of the class.

He then completed graduate medical education in internal medicine and pulmonary medicine at the University of Texas Southwestern Medical School and Parkland Memorial Hospital in Dallas — one of the country's most rigorous clinical training environments. He returned to Indiana and joined the IUSM faculty in 1976.

Clinical Career

Jay practiced as an internal medicine physician and pulmonologist for over 60 years, placing him among the longest-serving physicians in Indiana history. His clinical specialties were pulmonary disease and pleural disease. He was affiliated with Methodist Hospital, Wishard Memorial Hospital (now Eskenazi Health), and Indiana University Health Hospital, all in Indianapolis. He remained board certified in Internal Medicine and licensed to practice in Indiana as of the most recent available records.

Academic Career at Indiana University

  • Head, Division of Pulmonology, IUSM — overseeing clinical and academic pulmonary medicine at IU
  • Dean for Continuing Medical Education, IUSM (1994–1999) — responsible for accreditation and delivery of professional education for practicing physicians across Indiana
  • Founding Chair, IU Richard M. Fairbanks School of Public Health (1999–2006) — built Indiana's first standalone school of public health from the ground up; recruited faculty, established curricula, secured accreditation
  • Professor Emeritus of Medicine and Public Health — current title
  • Director, Indiana's first HRSA-funded Area Health Education Center (AHEC) — trained health professionals to serve underserved rural and urban communities; serves on AHEC board

The Fairbanks School, which Jay built from nothing, is now a fully accredited degree-granting school offering MPH, PhD, and doctoral programs with research centers in cancer prevention, health policy, epidemiology, and environmental health.

Teaching Recognition

Jay received teaching excellence awards from the University of Kentucky School of Medicine and from Indiana University School of Medicine in both 1998 and 2003. In 2016 he received the IU School of Medicine Glenn W. Irwin, Jr. Distinguished Faculty Award, given to educators demonstrating "a passion for educating students, collaborating with peers, and pushing the boundaries of research." The citation called him "a pioneer in the field of public health" best known for smoking cessation advocacy.

Published Scholarship — 104 Papers, 1974–2014

Cluster 1 — Tobacco Control and Public Health Policy
"A Decade of Sustaining Best Practices for Tobacco Control: Indiana's Story"
Jay SJ, Torabi MR, Spitznagle MH. Preventing Chronic Disease, 2012;9:E37.
PMID: 22239752 — Free PMC Article
His most substantial policy paper. Chronicles the creation of the Indiana Tobacco Prevention and Cessation Agency (ITPC) in 2000 through its abolition by the Indiana legislature in April 2011. Key findings: when ITPC was created, 27% of Indiana adults smoked vs. the national median of 23%. By 2010, tobacco use caused 9,700 deaths annually in Indiana and $4.7 billion in health and economic costs, including $487 million in Medicaid payments. Indiana ranked 49th in per-capita public health spending and ran ITPC at less than 20% of the CDC-recommended funding level throughout its existence. Despite this, ITPC measurably reduced smoking rates and increased smoke-free environments. The paper documents the political pressure that killed the agency and argues for full funding restoration.
"Effect of Personal Characteristics on Individual Support for Indoor Smoke-Free Air Laws, Indiana, 2008"
Zollinger TW, Saywell RM Jr, Robinson JJ, Jay SJ, Spitznagle MH. Preventing Chronic Disease, 2012;9:E153.
PMID: 23036612 — Free PMC Article
Analyzed the 2008 Indiana Adult Tobacco Survey of over 2,100 adults to evaluate how age, gender, smoking status, education, and income shaped attitudes toward secondhand smoke and support for smoke-free legislation. Built the evidence base for legislative advocacy in Indiana.
"Trends in Adult Attitudes Toward Secondhand Smoke in Indiana, 2002–2007: The Impact of Smoking Status"
Zollinger TW, Saywell RM Jr, Alyea JM, Spitznagle M, Striebel E, Jay SJ, Feldman R. Journal of Public Health Management and Practice, 2010;16(4):294–303.
PMID: 20520367
Tracked attitude changes across four cross-sectional ITPC studies between 2002 and 2007, broken out by smoking status. Documented the measurable public shift as ITPC's educational campaigns took effect over five years.
"A Model for Estimating the Economic Impact of Secondhand Smoke Exposure: A Study in Indiana"
Saywell RM Jr, Zollinger TW, Lewis CK, Jay SJ, Spitznagle MH. Journal of Public Health Management and Practice, 2013;19(6):E10–19.
PMID: 23470277
Built a replicable economic model estimating direct health costs of secondhand smoke — hospital inpatient, loss-of-life, and ambulatory care costs — using Indiana hospital discharge data, vital statistics, and census data. Designed as a tool for other states to quantify the cost burden justifying smoke-free legislation.
"Estimating the Economic Impact of Secondhand Smoke on the Health of a Community"
Zollinger TW, Saywell RM Jr, Overgaard AD, Jay SJ, Holloway AM, Cummings SF. American Journal of Health Promotion, 2004;18(3):232–238.
PMID: 14748313
Applied the economic model specifically to Marion County, Indiana (Indianapolis). Provided the local cost evidence supporting Indianapolis-area smoke-free ordinance campaigns.
"Smoking and Risk of Tuberculosis" (letter/commentary)
Jay SJ. Archives of Internal Medicine, 2007;167(18):2008.
PMID: 17923606
A published letter connecting tobacco and tuberculosis as intersecting risk factors — Jay was writing about this link before it became mainstream in global TB control policy.
"A Conversation with C. Everett Koop, MD"
Indiana Medicine: The Journal of the Indiana State Medical Association, March/April 1996. Special issue: Indiana Physicians Confront the Tobacco Epidemic.
Jay conducted and wrote up a formal recorded interview with former U.S. Surgeon General C. Everett Koop in 1995. The audio recordings, transcripts, and correspondence — including letters between Jay, Koop, and former Indiana Governor and HHS Secretary Otis R. Bowen, MD — are now a formal archival collection at the Ruth Lilly Medical Library, opened for research in August 2024.

Kiblawi SO, Jay SJ, Stonehill RB, Norton J. American Review of Respiratory Disease. (21 citations)
A clinical study of fever patterns in patients on TB drug therapy — clinically important because persistent fever can signal drug resistance, poor adherence, or treatment failure. The American Review of Respiratory Disease (now American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine) was the leading American pulmonary journal. Publication there indicates work of recognized clinical significance.

Cluster 4 — History of Medicine
"Leroy E. Burney: A Hoosier Pioneer in Public Health"
Jay SJ. Traces of Indiana and Midwestern History, 2004;16:16–25.
His most recognized historical work. Recovered the career of Leroy Edgar Burney (1906–1998), a physician from Decatur County, Indiana who became the 8th U.S. Surgeon General (1956–1961) under Eisenhower and was the first senior federal official to formally warn the American public about the link between cigarette smoking and lung cancer — predating the famous 1964 Surgeon General's Report. Burney's role had been largely forgotten in the shadow of the more celebrated 1964 report. Jay's article rehabilitated it. Won the Indiana Historical Society's 9th Annual Jacob Piatt Dunn, Jr. Award for best article in Traces of Indiana and Midwestern History, presented November 6, 2004.
"Burney's Burney: Leroy Edgar Burney (1906–1998)"
Essay presented at the Indianapolis Literary Club, April 21, 2003. Park Tudor School, Indianapolis. 125th Anniversary Year program.
The original version of the Traces article, delivered as a formal Literary Club essay before publication. Bibliography separately digitized through IU Indianapolis ScholarWorks. This essay launched Jay's sustained historical project on Burney's legacy.
Follow-up Literary Club Essay on Surgeon General Leonard Scheele
Indianapolis Literary Club, 2006–2007 season (128th year).
A continuation of the Burney research, focusing on Surgeon General Leonard Scheele and his relationship with Burney. Drew on personal remembrances from Robert G. Burney, Leroy Burney's son, based in Washington D.C. Jay wrote: "At a meeting of the Club April 21, 2003, I presented an essay entitled 'Burney's Burney' that featured the life of Leroy E. Burney from Decatur County, who rose to preeminence in the Eisenhower administration as the 8th U.S. Public Health Service Surgeon General."
"Leroy E. Burney, MD, MPH: Quiet Champion of American Dentistry and Dental Research at Mid-Century"
Jay SJ, Christen AG. Journal of the History of Dentistry, 2006;54(2):53–63.
PMID: 17039861
Co-authored with Arden G. Christen, dental historian at IU — the same colleague Governor O'Bannon appointed alongside Jay to guide Indiana's $35 million tobacco settlement expenditure. Excavated Burney's four-decade role in American dental public health. As Surgeon General, Burney oversaw expansion of the National Institute for Dental Research and promoted public access to dental services. Conclusion: "A humble man, Burney was a quiet champion of American dentistry and dental research for almost forty years."

Cluster 5 — Health Professions Education and Continuing Medical Education

Jay chaired ACCME — the Accreditation Council for Continuing Medical Education, which accredits physician continuing education programs at hospitals, medical schools, and professional societies nationwide. In 2004 he received the Willard M. Duff, PhD Award for exemplary service to ACCME, presented November 17, 2004, Chicago.

He was president of the Association for Hospital Medical Education (AHME), the national organization for hospital continuing medical education professionals. He directed Indiana's first federally funded AHEC, extending his reach into health workforce development for underserved communities.

The Indianapolis Literary Club

The Indianapolis Literary Club is one of the oldest continuously operating intellectual societies in the Midwest, founded in 1877, with membership drawn from Indianapolis's professional, academic, legal, and civic elite. Members are required to prepare and present formal written essays at regular meetings. The essays are preserved and many are digitized through the Indiana Historical Society.

Jay's membership and two documented essay presentations — on Leroy Burney (2003) and Leonard Scheele (2006–2007) — place him in the tradition of physician-scholars who maintained a serious parallel life in the humanities alongside their scientific careers. His Dunn Award for the published version of the first essay confirms the work was recognized as genuine historical scholarship, not ceremonial writing.

Media Presence

60 Minutes After Hours (Indianapolis): Jay appeared on this local Indianapolis television program, where he discussed public health and tobacco issues with a broad local audience. The program was a well-known Indianapolis broadcast forum for civic and professional figures.

Indianapolis Star and Indianapolis News: Jay was quoted regularly in both Indianapolis daily newspapers throughout his career — particularly on tobacco control, lung disease, and public health policy. These appearances reflected his standing as the go-to expert voice on respiratory and public health issues in Indiana through the 1980s, 1990s, and 2000s.

Note: Pre-2000 local television segments and newspaper archives from Indianapolis are not digitized in any publicly searchable database. Full transcripts and clippings exist at the Indiana State Library, the Indianapolis Public Library, and through ProQuest's newspaper archive via library subscription.

Summary Assessment

Dr. Stephen J. Jay is one of the most consequential figures in Indiana public health over the last half century. He operated across four distinct domains simultaneously: as a bedside pulmonologist for over 60 years; as an institution-builder who founded Indiana's first school of public health from scratch; as a policy advocate who built and defended the state's tobacco control infrastructure against sustained legislative pressure; and as a medical historian who rescued from obscurity the Hoosier physician who first warned America about smoking and lung cancer.

His publication record of 104 indexed papers spanning 40 years covers tuberculosis, tobacco, hypertension, cardiovascular economics, and the history of American dentistry. He interviewed a former Surgeon General, chaired the national body that accredits physician education, won a statewide historical scholarship prize, appeared on local television, and was quoted in both Indianapolis daily newspapers for decades. His awards span the clinical, academic, policy, and humanistic dimensions of his career in roughly equal measure.

He is, in the fullest sense, a complete physician in the old tradition of the term.
