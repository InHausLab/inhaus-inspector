We've done a handful of assessments in Aspen over the last few months — Wildcat Ranch, American Way, and a few others — and the pattern has been consistent: even very nice, newer homes often have things going on that nobody knew about.

Not scary things. Fixable things. Elevated copper in the water. Radon above the EPA threshold. Air quality or allergen issues that may help explain symptoms a family has been dealing with for years.

This is what clients receive after an assessment: a Home Health Score out of 100, with a category-by-category breakdown across air, water, mold, radon, allergens, and surfaces, plus a prioritized plan for what to address first.

The assessment takes about two hours on site, and the report is typically ready within a few days.

What has been interesting is that once people see the report, someone specific usually comes to mind — a new buyer who wants to understand the home beyond a standard inspection, a listing where a clean score could be a meaningful selling point, a family with kids or allergy issues, or someone who simply wants to know whether their home environment is as healthy as it should be.
